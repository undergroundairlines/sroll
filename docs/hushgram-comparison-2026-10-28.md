# Scroll Guard Fresh versus HushGram

For the experience you described—friends' posts, messaging, photo sending and Stories, with less opportunity to keep consuming recommended Reels—**HushGram has stronger internal Instagram controls than Scroll Guard currently has**. Scroll Guard keeps the official Instagram installation and also covers YouTube Shorts and TikTok. Neither has been verified on your particular Nothing OS phone in this comparison. I cannot substantiate a claim that Scroll Guard is the best Home-Reel blocker.

This review compares Scroll Guard Fresh 0.4.6, source `dd15e9b`, with HushGram's **released v0.0.16**, commit `1cf4d5628c6194ebccad015c4d33e022a77ed8e6`, published 24 October 2026. HushGram's main branch already describes unreleased 0.0.17; those additions are excluded. Its released catalog contains 117 patches, covering many settings rather than 117 independent blocking methods. [Release](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/releases/tag/v0.0.16), [released catalog](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/patches-list.json).

I inspected the source, catalog, release metadata and published verification receipt. I did not install HushGram, execute its patches, sign into an Instagram account, or test it on your phone. No HushGram code was copied into Scroll Guard.

The Home distinction matters. HushGram's `FeedReels` helper removes four recognised recommendation-unit kinds. A followed account's Reel can remain a normal media item. Its separate `FeedSuggestions` helper deals with individual suggestion units, and its Home media filter distinguishes photo, video and carousel types. Consequently, hiding suggested-Reel rows, hiding suggested posts and hiding every video are different choices. They should not be collapsed into one claim that every Home Reel disappears. [Reel-unit filter](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/reels/FeedReels.java), [suggestion and media-type filters](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/feed/FeedSuggestions.java).

Its whole-Home implementation filters both newly parsed items and cached Home items, while leaving other feeds alone. The Story tray is obtained separately. This is a useful example of handling cached content at its actual owner rather than looking for a convenient visible marker. Scroll Guard's stricter Home lock interrupts viewing through an external overlay instead. [Home filter](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/feed/HomeFeed.java), [injected Home hooks](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/patches/src/main/kotlin/app/morphe/patches/instagram/feed/home/HideHomeFeedPatch.kt).

HushGram's swipe restriction is more targeted than our shared-Reel shield: it records Reel pagers, disables their user input, intercepts later attempts to re-enable it and restricts the viewer's pull-to-refresh handling. Other pagers remain native. Its source explicitly permits programmatic auto-scroll, however, and runtime exceptions restore normal behavior. The optional quota counts forward page progression, not a verified account-independent total of unique videos; its session counters are held in process memory. [Runtime scrolling control](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/reels/ReelScrolling.java), [patch targets](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/patches/src/main/kotlin/app/morphe/patches/instagram/reels/scrolling/StopReelsScrollingPatch.kt).

The settings lock and scroll budget are separate mechanisms. The lock stores its wait, uses elapsed time during the same boot and falls back to wall-clock time after reboot. Scroll breaks use in-process timing and an internal view over selected distracting screens; messages and navigation are intended to stay usable. This is more flexibility than our fixed settings deadline, but is not evidence that every quota survives process termination. [Settings lock](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/settings/SettingsLock.java), [break implementation](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/extensions/instagram/src/main/java/app/hushgram/extension/instagram/reels/ScrollBreak.java).

Those families cover the released catalog's Ads, Ghost mode, Privacy, Messages, Feed, Stories, Reels, Playback, Interaction, Profiles, Interface, Downloads, Updates, Fixes and Settings categories. Most cosmetic, download and account-behavior modifications are outside this app's purpose. Keeping auto-scroll enabled would work against your stated goal. [Catalog](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/patches-list.json).

For installation, HushGram uses Morphe Manager on the phone and a supported Instagram APK; a computer and root are not inherently required. Its normal replacement route needs re-signing and a fresh Instagram login. It also documents an optional separately packaged Instagram copy. Scroll Guard Fresh is a separate APK and leaves the official Instagram installation unchanged. HushGram's inspected release targets Instagram 452.1.0.53.64; other versions are not established as compatible. Scroll Guard can keep working across stock updates, but interface changes can also break its detection. [Installation and supported builds](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/README.md#install).

HushGram's project warns that a modified Instagram may be detectable and may expose the account to enforcement; this comparison does not establish a ban rate or a guarantee of account safety. Scroll Guard does not re-sign Instagram or handle its login. Its accessibility permission is nevertheless broad and sensitive. HushGram says its extension has no collection server, while its requested media saves use Meta's network infrastructure. Scroll Guard declares no Internet permission and keeps its diagnostics local. Neither statement means Instagram itself stops collecting data. In particular, old claims about HushGram disabling analytics are outdated: that feature was removed in its 0.0.15 changelog. [Privacy disclosure](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/README.md#privacy), [change history](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/CHANGELOG.md).

For background reliability, HushGram's controls run inside Instagram instead of depending on a separate accessibility service. Scroll Guard must remain connected to Android's accessibility framework, including under Nothing OS background management. This architectural difference is meaningful; I have no controlled battery, latency or reliability benchmark establishing a winner on your phone.

For verification, HushGram provides real-APK patch tests and a release receipt tying its bundle to a specific arm64 Instagram build, with applied-patch and manifest results. Its fixture-dependent tests can skip without the actual APK inputs. The fetched v0.0.16 receipt is patch-time evidence, not a record proving every messaging/Story/swipe behavior on Nothing OS. The later main branch describes additional release-walk tooling; that must not be credited retrospectively to this published receipt. Scroll Guard Fresh passed 194 unit tests, lint, APK compilation and 42 Android 15 synthetic instrumentation tests, with zero failures/errors/skips, in [this run](https://github.com/undergroundairlines/sroll/actions/runs/40511890795). The emulator suite uses a synthetic Instagram fixture and actual accessibility/overlay/touch dispatch. That proves the tested Android mechanics and guard transitions, not identification on real Instagram. I have not rerun HushGram's test suite. [Published receipt](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/releases/download/v0.0.16/release-receipt-0.0.16.json), [fixture instructions](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/README.md#building-from-source).

The release receipt records 116 applied patches for its inspected main-package target; the 117-entry catalog also lists the optional separate-install patch, absent from that target's applied list. The receipt alone therefore does not establish that cloning route's runtime behavior. Neither a patch count nor our test count is a meaningful standalone quality ranking.

The most useful work for Scroll Guard is, in order: prove current real-Instagram Home/chat/Story behavior with privacy-safe structural samples; separate Home-media, full-Home, Explore and Reel choices; improve intentional shared-Reel interaction only where its enforcement can be demonstrated; then consider interruption budgets, settings search and export. The native filtering and pager hooks cannot simply be pasted into a separate accessibility app. Matching those capabilities may require choosing a patched client or another architecture. HushGram is GPL-3.0 and Scroll Guard is Apache-2.0; code reuse would also need a deliberate licensing review. [HushGram license](https://github.com/SysAdminDoc/HushGram/blob/v0.0.16/LICENSE).

For your stated preference, HushGram is closer in available Instagram-specific controls. Scroll Guard is the less invasive installation choice for the official Instagram app and already addresses your YouTube/TikTok requirements. That is the supported distinction; a universal “best” verdict would go beyond the evidence.
